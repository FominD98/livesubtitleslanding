Steam Game Subtitles & AI Translation for PC Games

Steam's catalogue runs into the hundreds of thousands of games, and a substantial slice of it — especially Japanese RPGs, Korean RPGs and visual novels, indie story-driven titles, and obscure simulation games — ships without subtitle support, without an English voice track, or without any localisation at all. For players who love niche genres, that is a wall: the audio is the story, and the story is in a language you do not read. Steam game subtitles via Live Subtitles knock the wall down. The app captions and translates game audio in real time on Windows, with a Game Mode overlay that stays on top of fullscreen titles and a recognition engine tuned for the languages anime fans, JRPG players, and visual-novel readers actually need.

How to add live subtitles to Steam games in 3 steps

  1. Download Live Subtitles from the Microsoft Store and launch it before starting the game.
  2. Select "System Audio" and enable Game Mode so the overlay stays above fullscreen games.
  3. Launch any Steam game — captions appear in a floating overlay you can move and resize.

Built for story-driven games without subtitle support

Some of the most beloved games on Steam — long-running JRPG series, Type-Moon visual novels, NieR-style narrative titles, indie experiments — were authored in Japanese or Korean and never received an official English subtitle pass for parts of the dialogue. Fan translations exist for some, but not all, and they take years. Live Subtitles fills that gap on launch day: the original voice acting is preserved, the dialogue is transcribed in the source language, and a translation appears below it in your chosen target language.

Use cases that work especially well:

  • Japanese RPGs, action games, and visual novels — translate Japanese voice into English live.
  • Korean MMORPGs and visual novels with rich Korean voice acting.
  • Mandarin and Cantonese indie titles from Chinese developers.
  • European titles in German, French, Italian, or Russian that lack English audio dubs.
  • Older games that shipped with audio in one language and no localisation patch.

Game Mode keeps the overlay on top

Steam games typically run in exclusive fullscreen or borderless windowed mode, which traditionally hides the Windows desktop and any third-party overlays. Live Subtitles ships a dedicated Game Mode that keeps the caption overlay visible above the game window in either mode. You can resize the overlay, dock it to a corner of the screen, change the font and background opacity, and move it anywhere that does not block your HUD. For ultrawide and dual-monitor setups, the overlay can live on a secondary screen entirely.

Safe with Steam, anti-cheat, and online play

Live Subtitles never touches the game process. It does not inject DLLs, does not read or write game memory, does not modify save files, and does not interact with any game APIs. It simply reads the audio output buffer at the Windows mixer — the same buffer that powers your speakers and headphones. Anti-cheat systems including Easy Anti-Cheat, BattlEye, and Vanguard see nothing to act on, because Live Subtitles is invisible to the game. Online play, achievements, trading cards, and Steam Family Sharing all work normally.

Accessibility for hearing-impaired and language-learning gamers

For gamers with hearing loss, the missing-subtitle problem is not optional — there are titles that are simply unplayable without captions. Live Subtitles also doubles as a language-learning tool: visual-novel and JRPG players who study Japanese can show original Japanese speech on top and an English translation below, building reading and listening fluency through games they actually enjoy. Supported recognition and translation languages include English, Japanese, Korean, Mandarin Chinese, Cantonese, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian, Polish, and 40+ more.
