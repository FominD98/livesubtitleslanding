Translate Any Video on Your Android Phone

Live Subtitles translates video playing on your Android phone, live, in a floating overlay — a line of the original spoken language plus your translation, in 50+ languages. It reads the phone's audio directly, so it works the same way regardless of which app the video is open in, not just one platform.

One app, any video source

Instead of a translator built into a single platform, Live Subtitles sits on top of whatever's playing:

  • Video in a mobile browser — news sites, blogs, embedded players.
  • YouTube, in the app or a browser.
  • Downloaded video files played from the gallery or a file manager.
  • Podcasts and audio-only content.
  • Most short-form and streaming apps (see the caveat below for DRM-protected services).

How it works

System Audio mode

Pick System Audio and Live Subtitles reads whatever the phone is playing, through Android's screen/audio capture API. Android shows a one-time capture-consent prompt each session — a standard requirement for any app that reads screen/audio output, not something specific to Live Subtitles.

Dual subtitles, 50+ languages

The original spoken line and your chosen translation appear together, in any of 50+ languages, in either direction — not limited to translating into or out of English.

A floating overlay

Captions sit on top of the video, draggable and resizable, and stay in place as you switch between apps.

Setup: a few steps, once

  1. Install Live Subtitles from Google Play. Open it and try it free — no account required.
  2. Grant "Display over other apps." The app opens this Android settings screen for you the first time.
  3. Pick System Audio as the source, and set your spoken and translation languages.
  4. Play the video, in whichever app it's in. The overlay translates it live.
What System Audio can't capture: DRM-protected streaming audio from services like Netflix, Prime Video, and Disney+ — Android mutes that audio for any capture, by design, so no app can read it directly. The same applies to live video calls and voice chats in apps like Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, WhatsApp, Telegram, or Discord — Android routes that audio separately from media playback. For either case, switch to Microphone mode: point the phone's mic at the speaker and Live Subtitles captions and translates what it hears instead. It won't be quite as clean as a direct audio feed, but it works.

FAQ

What kinds of video can it translate?
Anything playing as ordinary media audio: browser video, YouTube, downloaded files, podcasts, livestreams, most streaming apps.

Does it work in any language, in any direction?
Yes — 50+ languages each way, not limited to English pairs.

Does it work with Netflix, Prime Video, or other DRM-protected apps?
No, not through System Audio — Android blocks that for any app. Use Microphone mode instead.

Does it translate video calls?
Not through System Audio — Android keeps call audio separate. Use Microphone mode for a call on speaker.

Is there a dual-language view?
Yes — original and translated line shown together.

Do I need an internet connection?
Yes — speech recognition and translation run in the cloud.

Can I try it for free?
Yes. New installs get free minutes, no credit card required.
