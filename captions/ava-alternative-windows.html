Ava alternative for Windows: captions for any sound on your PC

Updated October 27, 2026

Ava is primarily a mobile app, built around captioning in-person group conversations on phones. If your calls, videos and lectures happen on a Windows computer, Live Captions & Transcribe is a closer fit: it captions any sound playing on a Windows 10 or 11 PC (or the microphone) in a window on top of other apps, shows each speaker in their own color and saves a transcript you can export to TXT or SRT.

These two apps are built for different situations, and plenty of people could use both. Below is an honest look at where each one makes sense, so you can pick by how you actually spend your day rather than by a feature list.

When Ava is the better choice

  • Face-to-face conversations away from a computer. A dinner, a family gathering, a meeting room where everyone has a phone: a mobile app built for group conversations is made for this.
  • You want captions in your pocket. If the phone is the device you always have with you, a phone app wins on convenience.
  • Your group already uses Ava. If colleagues or family are set up on it, it makes sense to stay where they are.

When Live Captions & Transcribe fits better

Your conversations happen on the computer

Live Captions & Transcribe listens to the sound your PC plays. That covers calls in Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, WhatsApp desktop, Discord and Telegram desktop, and also YouTube, Netflix, podcasts and recorded lectures. You don't need a separate setup for each app: if you can hear it on the PC, you can read it. Captions appear about one second after the words are spoken, in a window that stays on top.

You use Windows 10

The app runs on both Windows 10 and Windows 11 (x64). The built-in Live Captions in Windows exists only on Windows 11, version 22H2 and later, so on an older PC a separate app is the way to get captions for everything. More on that in our guide to live captions on Windows 10.

You want to keep the text

Every session is saved in a history on your PC. Search it by word, rename "Speaker 1" and "Speaker 2" to real names, copy a quote, or export the whole thing to TXT for notes or SRT for subtitles. This is handy for lectures, long meetings and interviews you'll come back to.

You need to follow several speakers

Each speaker gets their own color and starts on a new line, so a fast discussion doesn't turn into one block of text.

You want big, clear text on a big screen

Text goes up to 64 pt, with light, dark and high-contrast styles. Turn on click-through and your mouse clicks pass through the caption window to the app underneath. Large text like this also helps people who are hard of hearing follow family video calls.

How to set up Live Captions & Transcribe

  1. Install it. Open the Live Captions & Transcribe page in the Microsoft Store and click Get. No account or card is needed to try it.
  2. Choose what to listen to. Computer sound for calls, videos and lectures; the microphone for people speaking in the room.
  3. Press Start. The language is detected automatically from 60 speech languages.
  4. Adjust the window. Move it next to the call or under the video, set the text size and style, and turn on click-through if you like.
  5. Review afterwards. Open the history to search, rename speakers, copy, or export to TXT or SRT.

Good to know. The app needs an internet connection. While captions are running, the audio is sent to a cloud speech recognition service; it is not recorded, and transcripts stay on your PC. Captions are in the spoken language only, with no translation. If you need translation, use the sister app Live Subtitles.

Can you use both?

Yes, and for some people that's the most practical answer: a phone app for conversations around the table, and Live Captions & Transcribe on the computer for calls, videos and lectures, with a transcript you can keep.

Questions and answers

Is Live Captions & Transcribe a replacement for Ava?
It depends on where you need captions. Ava is primarily a mobile app built around in-person group conversations. Live Captions & Transcribe is a Windows app for Windows 10 and 11 that captions any sound playing on the PC, or the microphone. If most of your conversations happen on the computer, it can replace a phone app there; for face-to-face groups on phones, Ava may still fit better.
Does Live Captions & Transcribe work on Windows 10?
Yes. It runs on Windows 10 and Windows 11 (x64) and is installed from the Microsoft Store.
Does it caption Zoom, Teams and WhatsApp calls on the PC?
Yes. It listens to the sound your PC plays, so it captions calls in Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, WhatsApp desktop, Discord and Telegram desktop, as well as YouTube, Netflix, podcasts and lectures. Nothing is installed into those apps, no bot joins the call, and the other people do not see your captions. See captions for WhatsApp, Teams and Telegram calls.
Can I save and export the transcript?
Yes. Each session is saved in a history on your PC, where you can search it, rename speakers, copy the text or export it to TXT or SRT.
Does it work offline?
No. It needs an internet connection. While captions are running, the audio is sent to a cloud speech recognition service. The audio is not recorded, and transcripts stay on your PC. Details are in the privacy policy.
How much does it cost?
You can try it free without an account or card. After that you buy minute packs or a subscription; current prices are on the Live Captions page.
