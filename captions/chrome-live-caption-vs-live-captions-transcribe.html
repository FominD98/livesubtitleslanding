Chrome Live Caption vs a system-wide captions app

Updated October 27, 2026

Chrome Live Caption is free and works well if everything you want to caption plays in a Chrome tab. It does not hear anything outside the browser and does not save what was said. A system-wide app such as Live Captions & Transcribe captions any sound on a Windows 10 or 11 PC, including the Zoom, Teams, WhatsApp and Telegram desktop apps, and keeps a transcript you can export to TXT or SRT.

The real question is where your audio comes from. A YouTube video or a Google Meet call in the browser is one thing; a Teams desktop call, a lecture recording in a media player or a WhatsApp call from the desktop app is another.

When Chrome Live Caption is enough

  • You mostly watch YouTube or other videos in Chrome.
  • You join Google Meet, Zoom or Teams in a Chrome tab rather than in the desktop app.
  • You only need to read along; you don't need the text afterwards.
  • You want something free that's already on your PC.

To turn it on, open Chrome, go to Settings → Accessibility (or type chrome://settings/accessibility in the address bar) and switch on Live Caption.

When you need a system-wide app

Your calls are in desktop apps

The Zoom and Teams desktop apps, WhatsApp desktop, Telegram desktop and Discord all play sound outside Chrome, so Chrome Live Caption never hears them. Live Captions & Transcribe listens to the PC's sound output, so it doesn't matter which app is talking. Nothing is installed into those apps, no bot joins the call, and the other people don't see your captions. Step by step: captions for WhatsApp, Teams and Telegram calls on a PC.

You want the text afterwards

Every session goes into a history on your PC. Search it by word, rename "Speaker 1" and "Speaker 2" to real names, copy a quote, or export it to TXT for notes or SRT for subtitles. Useful for lectures, webinars and long meetings.

Several people are talking

Each speaker gets their own color and starts on a new line, which makes a busy meeting much easier to follow than one stream of text.

You switch between apps all day

One caption window stays on top of everything: a Teams call in the morning, a course video in a media player at lunch, Netflix in the evening. Text goes up to 64 pt, with light, dark and high-contrast styles, and click-through lets your mouse clicks pass to the app underneath.

How to start with Live Captions & Transcribe

  1. Install Live Captions & Transcribe from the Microsoft Store. No account or card is needed to try it.
  2. Choose computer sound for calls, videos and lectures, or the microphone for people in the room.
  3. Press Start. Captions appear about one second after the words are spoken; the language is detected automatically.
  4. After the session, open the history to search, rename speakers, copy or export to TXT or SRT.

If you keep Chrome Live Caption switched on, turn it off while the app is running so you don't read the same words twice.

Good to know. Live Captions & Transcribe needs an internet connection. While captions are running, the audio is sent to a cloud speech recognition service; it is not recorded, and transcripts stay on your PC. Captions are in the spoken language only; the app does not translate. For translation, use the sister app Live Subtitles.

Questions and answers

Does Chrome Live Caption work with the Zoom or Teams desktop app?
No. Chrome Live Caption captions only audio that plays in Chrome. If you join Zoom, Teams or Google Meet in a Chrome tab, it can caption the call; the desktop apps are outside the browser, so it does not hear them. A system-wide app such as Live Captions & Transcribe captions the sound of the whole PC.
Can Chrome Live Caption save a transcript?
No. Chrome Live Caption shows captions while audio plays but does not keep a history or export the text. Live Captions & Transcribe saves each session on your PC with search, speaker renaming, copy and TXT/SRT export.
Is Chrome Live Caption free?
Yes, it is part of Chrome. Live Captions & Transcribe is free to try without an account or card; after that you buy minute packs or a subscription (see prices).
Does Live Captions & Transcribe work on Windows 10?
Yes. It runs on Windows 10 and Windows 11 (x64) and is installed from the Microsoft Store.
Does Live Captions & Transcribe need the internet?
Yes. While captions are running, the audio is sent to a cloud speech recognition service to turn it into text. The audio is not recorded, and transcripts are saved only on your PC. Details are in the privacy policy.
Should I use both?
You can, but not at the same time for the same audio, or you will read two sets of captions. A sensible split is Chrome Live Caption for a quick browser video and Live Captions & Transcribe for calls, desktop apps and anything you want to keep as a transcript.
