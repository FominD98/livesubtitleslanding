Chinese to English Live Captions — Real-Time AI Translation

Chinese is the most spoken language in the world by native speakers, and the gap between Chinese and English creates one of the most common translation challenges in global business, streaming, and everyday communication. Chinese to English live captions bridge that gap instantly — Live Subtitles transcribes Chinese speech and translates it to English in real time, displaying both languages on screen at once.

Whether you are on a business call with a Chinese supplier, watching a Chinese drama on Bilibili, or helping a Mandarin-speaking family member communicate, Live Subtitles works with any app on your Windows PC without plugins, bots, or configuration on the other end.

How to set up Chinese to English live captions in 3 steps

  1. Download Live Subtitles from the Microsoft Store and launch the app.
  2. Set recognition to "Chinese (Simplified)" for Mandarin, "Chinese (Traditional)" for Taiwan/HK, or "Cantonese" — then set translation to "English".

Mandarin and Cantonese — both supported

Chinese is not a single spoken language — Mandarin (Putonghua) and Cantonese (Yue Chinese) are distinct spoken varieties with different pronunciation, vocabulary, and rhythm. Live Subtitles recognizes both:

  • Chinese (Simplified) — Mandarin: Standard Mainland Chinese as spoken in Beijing, Shanghai, and across the PRC
  • Chinese (Traditional) — Mandarin/Taiwan: Traditional character set used in Taiwan and parts of Hong Kong; recognizes standard Taiwan Mandarin speech
  • Cantonese: Yue Chinese as spoken in Guangdong province, Hong Kong, and large diaspora communities worldwide

You can switch between recognition languages at any time in the app settings, making it easy to go from a Mandarin business call to a Cantonese family video chat without restarting the app.

Learn Chinese with dual subtitles

For Chinese language learners, dual subtitle mode is one of the most powerful study tools available. Instead of watching Chinese content with English-only subtitles — which causes your brain to stop engaging with the Chinese — you see both simultaneously:

  • The top line shows the original Chinese text (characters or pinyin-based display)
  • The bottom line shows the English translation in real time
  • Your comprehension improves naturally through repeated exposure with immediate meaning reinforcement

This method works whether you are a beginner learning to recognize tones and characters or an intermediate learner who wants to move beyond textbook sentences into authentic native-speed speech.
